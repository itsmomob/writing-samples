SAMPLE 01

Korea Femtech Summit 2026 — Recap Redesign

English Blog post / event recap Editorial redesign & rewrite Work sample
1 Why I chose this topic

This was a deliberate work sample. I chose it because it sits at the intersection of everything Vespexx does: femtech, Korea, global expansion, and community building.

The original recap had strong content but presented it as a single column of text — no visual hierarchy, no navigation, and the Femtech Korea membership CTA buried at the bottom. I wanted to show what the same content looks like when it is structured for a global audience: people who scan, who care about market numbers, and who need a reason to join the community.

I also wanted to demonstrate that I can take an existing asset and make it work harder without changing the facts. Every number, quote, and speaker reference is preserved from the original.

2 Target audience
  • Primary: North American founders, clinicians, investors, and women's health leaders evaluating whether Korea is a serious femtech market.
  • Secondary: Potential Femtech Korea members and KFS2027 partners.
  • Tertiary: Vespexx's existing community and summit attendees.
3 Performance / reaction

FILL: Work sample — not yet published. Once live, I would track scroll depth, time on page, CTA click-through to membership, and social shares. The page is built so individual sections can be pulled as shortform content for LinkedIn and X — which is exactly how the role describes the blog's relationship to social.

What this demonstrates

  • Editorial judgment: structuring a long recap so it is scannable without losing narrative flow.
  • Visual hierarchy: pull quotes, stat cards, a timeline, and a panel grid replace flat paragraphs.
  • Global framing: added North America-facing context at key moments — Soonr launching in North America, the investability of Korean teams.
  • Conversion focus: the Femtech Korea membership and KFS2027 CTA now has its own full-width section.
  • Technical execution: hand-coded HTML, CSS, and vanilla JS. No framework. Deployed via GitHub Pages.

SAMPLE 02

The Fangirls Made the Beatles

English Longform editorial Writer & researcher FILL: Published / Unpublished
1 Why I chose this topic

I studied fandom during my Master's, and one of the most interesting arguments I developed was that the Beatles would never have been the Beatles without the fangirls. I wanted to write a piece that took female enthusiasm seriously — not as a punchline, but as cultural infrastructure.

The piece is built on a personal chain: my father discovered the Beatles because a girl loved them. I discovered the Beatles because my father loved them. That chain is the whole argument. Fandom is not passive consumption. It is transmission.

I also wanted to write something that was fun to read. The subject matter is academic, but the tone is conversational. That balance — rigorous but not stiff — is what I would bring to Vespexx's blog.

2 Target audience
  • Primary: Music and culture readers, especially those interested in the Beatles, fandom, and women's cultural history.
  • Secondary: Readers interested in fandom studies, parasocial relationships, and the sociology of taste.
  • Tertiary: Anyone who has ever been dismissed for liking something "too much."
3 Performance / reaction

FILL: publication, date, page views, shares, notable comments, any syndication. If unpublished, say so plainly: this is a self-contained editorial sample, not a commissioned piece.

What this demonstrates

  • Argument: a clear thesis developed across a long piece, with evidence and counterargument.
  • Research: seven cited academic and journalistic sources on Beatlemania, fandom, and women's cultural participation.
  • Voice: conversational, funny, and precise — the register a North American blog audience expects.
  • Cultural translation: takes an academic subject and makes it accessible without dumbing it down.
  • Relevance: this is a piece about how communities form around shared enthusiasm — which is exactly what Femtech Korea is building.

SAMPLE 03

AI 시대에 우리가 영어를 더 '인간답게' 써야 하는 이유

Korean Shortform marketing post Writer & content strategist Best-performing post in current role
Note: This piece is in Korean. It is included as evidence of performance and audience instinct, with an English rendering below. My English longform samples are Samples 01 and 02.
1 Why I chose this topic

This was not a topic I was assigned. I chose it because I noticed two things happening at once: AI translation tools were getting genuinely good, and the people most anxious about it were the ones who had the most to gain from learning English anyway.

The objection I kept hearing was "이제 영어 공부 안 해도 되는 거 아냐?" — and I realized that if I did not answer that objection directly, no amount of feature promotion would land. So I wrote the piece that answers the objection first and sells second.

The argument is simple: AI transfers information, but it cannot transfer trust, warmth, or timing. The examples are deliberately concrete — eye contact with a business partner, "I totally get you" said at the right moment — because abstract claims about human connection do not move anyone. Specific moments do.

2 Target audience
  • Primary: Gangnam-area professionals in their 20s and 30s who are curious about English study groups but have not committed — the exact people who would use AI as a reason not to start.
  • Secondary: Existing study group members who share posts with friends.
  • Tertiary: Search traffic around "AI 번역기," "영어회화 스터디," and "강남 영어 스터디."
3 Performance / reaction

FILL: views, engagement, comment count, inquiry or sign-up lift, time period. This is my best-performing post in my current role.

The reaction that mattered most was not the view count. It was that readers responded to the argument, not the promotion. The piece worked because it took the reader's objection seriously instead of dismissing it — the same approach I would bring to writing about femtech for a North American audience that is skeptical, informed, and not easily impressed.

Original — Korean

요즘 AI 번역기나 통역 이어폰 성능, 정말 놀랍죠?

"이제 영어 공부 안 해도 되는 거 아냐?"라는 생각이 드는 것도 당연해요.

하지만 강남 직장인 영어 회화 스터디 랭스영이 내린 결론은 확고합니다. AI는 '정보'를 전달할 수 있지만, 사람 사이의 '신뢰'와 '온도'는 전달할 수 없거든요.

비즈니스 파트너와 눈을 맞추며 건네는 위트 한마디, 친구의 고민에 진심으로 공감하며 내뱉는 "I totally get you"는 기계가 대신해 줄 수 없는 영역이에요.

강남역 근처에서 영어를 쓸 기회는 생각보다 적지만, 우리가 영어를 배우는 진짜 이유는 '기계 없이도 온전한 나로서 소통하기 위해서'죠.

강남역 영어회화 스터디 랭스영은 단순히 문장을 만드는 법을 가르치지 않아요. AI는 절대 따라 할 수 없는 '사람 냄새 나는 대화의 기술'을 연습하는 곳이죠.

English rendering

These days, AI translators and interpretation earbuds are genuinely impressive. It is only natural to wonder: "Do I even need to study English anymore?"

But the conclusion we reached at 랭스영, a Gangnam English conversation study group, is firm. AI can deliver information. It cannot deliver trust or warmth between people.

The wit you exchange while making eye contact with a business partner. The "I totally get you" you say when a friend needs to hear it. These are not things a machine can do for you.

Near Gangnam Station, the chances to actually use English are fewer than you would think. The real reason we learn it is to communicate as ourselves, fully, without a machine in between.

랭스영 does not just teach you how to build sentences. It is a place to practice a kind of conversation AI can never imitate — one that still smells like a person.

What this demonstrates

  • Objection-first writing: the piece names the reader's real hesitation in line two and answers it before it sells anything. Same skill Vespexx needs for a skeptical North American audience.
  • Concrete over abstract: "trust and warmth" would be empty. Eye contact and "I totally get you" are not.
  • Soft CTA discipline: the post earns attention before it asks for anything.
  • Audience instinct: I read the room before I wrote, and the performance data confirms the read.
  • Proof of performance: included as evidence, not as an English longform sample.
